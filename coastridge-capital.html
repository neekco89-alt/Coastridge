Market Perspective · 01

Why the Lower Middle Market Remains an Attractive Investment Opportunity in 2026

Business quality and execution — not multiple expansion — are increasingly what separates strong outcomes from the rest.

The lower middle market continues to offer a compelling environment for disciplined private equity investors. Thousands of founder-led, family-owned, and entrepreneur-operated companies have established defensible positions within specialized industries but have not yet developed the institutional infrastructure, management depth, or access to capital needed to achieve their full potential. For investors capable of contributing strategic resources beyond capital alone, these businesses can present meaningful opportunities for long-term value creation.

The market is also becoming increasingly competitive. Axial reported that 2,635 new buyers joined its lower-middle-market platform during 2025, a 36% increase from the prior year. At the same time, acquisition multiples across private equity remained elevated, reinforcing the need for disciplined sourcing, careful underwriting, and a clearly defined value-creation thesis rather than reliance on favorable entry pricing or multiple expansion. (Axial)

In this environment, investment outcomes are likely to be driven increasingly by business quality and execution. Attractive companies typically exhibit durable customer relationships, differentiated products or services, recurring or predictable revenue, strong cash generation, defensible market positions, and capable leadership teams — along with identifiable opportunities to accelerate organic growth, strengthen operations, expand leadership capabilities, improve financial infrastructure, or pursue complementary acquisitions.

Add-on acquisitions are expected to remain a significant component of private equity activity in 2026 as investors seek geographic expansion, additional capabilities, operating efficiencies, and greater market density. However, a successful buy-and-build strategy requires more than completing transactions — it requires disciplined target selection, conservative underwriting, a repeatable integration process, and continued investment in the underlying platform company. (CLA)

The private investment firm model can be particularly well suited to the lower middle market. Without the pressure to deploy capital from a traditional blind-pool fund, independent sponsors can evaluate each opportunity individually and assemble the appropriate combination of equity capital, financing, industry expertise, and operating resources around a specific investment. This flexibility can support creative transaction structures and stronger alignment with founders, management teams, and investment partners.

At Coastridge Capital Partners, we believe the most attractive opportunities are found where a high-quality business can benefit from thoughtful capital, institutional discipline, and an engaged strategic partner. Our objective is not simply to acquire businesses, but to work alongside management teams to strengthen their organizations, execute clearly defined growth initiatives, and build more scalable, resilient, and strategically valuable enterprises.

Capital Markets · 02

Private Credit's Expanding Role in Middle-Market Transactions

Flexibility from direct lenders shouldn't be mistaken for inexpensive or unrestricted capital.

Private credit has evolved from an alternative financing source into a core component of the modern middle-market capital structure. Direct lenders now compete alongside commercial banks, broadly syndicated loan providers, specialty finance companies, insurance companies, and other institutional capital sources to finance acquisitions, recapitalizations, refinancings, and growth initiatives.

The global private credit market now manages more than $2 trillion and is projected by PwC to reach approximately $3.4 trillion by 2030. Its growth has been supported by institutional demand for private-market yield, regulatory constraints affecting traditional banks, and borrowers' preference for financing solutions that can provide greater flexibility and execution certainty. (PwC)

For lower-middle-market companies and financial sponsors, private credit can offer several strategic advantages. Direct lenders may provide customized structures, higher leverage capacity, delayed-draw acquisition facilities, unitranche financing, flexible amortization, and greater certainty around closing — capabilities that can be particularly valuable in acquisitions, corporate carve-outs, complex ownership transitions, or aggressive growth plans.

Flexibility, however, should not be confused with inexpensive or unrestricted capital. Private credit borrowers may face higher interest costs, tighter documentation, substantial call protection, financial maintenance covenants, reporting requirements, and limitations on acquisitions, distributions, and additional indebtedness. As competition within the asset class grows, regulators and investors are also paying greater attention to underwriting standards, valuation practices, transparency, and borrower performance. In August 2026, the Federal Reserve Banks of New York and Dallas announced plans for a pilot survey designed to improve visibility into credit availability and lending standards across the private credit market. (Reuters)

The appropriate financing solution should therefore be evaluated within the context of the broader investment strategy. Maximum leverage is not necessarily optimal leverage. A durable capital structure should provide adequate liquidity, preserve the company's ability to invest through economic cycles, and support strategic initiatives without creating an excessive burden on cash flow.

At Coastridge, we view debt financing as an important component of transaction strategy and long-term value creation. We seek to balance cost, flexibility, leverage, covenant structure, and execution certainty while aligning the financing package with the company's operating profile and strategic plan. The objective is not simply to fund the closing, but to establish a capital structure capable of supporting the business throughout the investment period.

Value Creation · 03

Beyond Financial Engineering: The Growing Importance of Operational Value Creation

With rate and multiple tailwinds gone, returns increasingly have to be built, not bought.

The private equity return environment has fundamentally changed. For many years, declining interest rates, readily available leverage, and expanding valuation multiples provided meaningful support to investment returns. Those conditions are no longer reliable assumptions. McKinsey's 2026 private-markets analysis describes an increasingly mature private equity industry in which the historical tailwinds of falling rates, multiple expansion, and abundant leverage have weakened. (McKinsey & Company)

As a result, investors must create a greater share of returns through improvements in the underlying business. Operational execution, organic growth, leadership development, technology adoption, strategic acquisitions, working-capital improvement, and disciplined capital allocation are increasingly central to the investment thesis. Recent private equity outlooks similarly point to a greater emphasis on operational value creation, digital transformation, and artificial-intelligence-enabled efficiencies. (CBH)

Effective value creation begins before an investment closes. During underwriting, investors should identify the specific initiatives expected to drive growth, improve profitability, reduce risk, or enhance strategic value — supported by realistic assumptions, accountable owners, measurable performance indicators, sufficient resources, and a practical implementation timeline.

For lower-middle-market companies, the greatest opportunities are often foundational. A business may have strong customer relationships, an attractive market position, and an entrepreneurial management team but lack scalable financial reporting, formal strategic planning, institutional sales processes, modern technology, or sufficient management depth. Addressing these needs can materially improve performance while positioning the company for sustainable growth.

Data quality is also becoming increasingly important. Operational initiatives can fail when management lacks accurate information regarding customer profitability, product costs, labor efficiency, pricing, or working-capital requirements. Better financial and operational visibility allows companies to prioritize initiatives, allocate resources more effectively, and measure whether the value-creation plan is producing the intended results. (Plante Moran)

At Coastridge, we believe value creation should strengthen the business rather than focus solely on short-term financial outcomes. Our approach is developed in partnership with management and tailored to the circumstances of each investment. By combining strategic planning, operating discipline, appropriate capitalization, and active governance, we seek to build companies with stronger leadership, improved performance, greater resilience, and broader long-term strategic options.
